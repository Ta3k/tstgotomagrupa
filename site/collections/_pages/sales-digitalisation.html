The situation we usually begin with

Companies rarely say that they want to digitalise sales. They describe specifics: no one knows what happens to an enquiry after it arrives. A salesperson keeps everything in a spreadsheet and in their head, so when they leave, the contact history remains in their inbox. Marketing brings in traffic, but linking it to orders comes down to guesswork.

This is rarely one problem solved by one tool. Part of it lies in a process that has never been documented. Part lies in a system the team uses differently from what was planned. And part lies in customer data scattered across three places, complete in none of them.

Enquiries get lost along the way

Contacts arrive through several channels and reach different people. There is no single place showing the stage of each case or who should take the next step.

The system exists, but sales happens outside it

The CRM was purchased and launched, yet the team follows its own methods. Data is entered later, selectively or not at all, so reports do not reflect reality.

You cannot see what actually brings in customers

The company pays for campaigns, a website and online visibility, but cannot trace which activities lead to an enquiry and then an order.

What this area covers across the Group

For us, sales digitalisation combines three things: how a company acquires contacts, how it manages them and what happens to an order after it is signed. We begin with the process, not the system, because a tool selected for an undefined process usually reproduces the same disorder in a new interface.

The sales process and its system support

We define the path from enquiry to order and assign responsibility at every stage. Only then do we check whether your existing system can be adapted or whether another would be a better fit.

Generating enquiries

The website, content, online presence and campaigns that lead a contact to a form or phone call. We treat this together with sales because both serve the same funnel at different stages.

Connection with the rest of the company

An order does not end with the sales team. It moves to fulfilment, the warehouse and accounting. With a broader scope, the project also covers this handover so data does not have to be entered again.

Where AI fits in

Sales is where the question about AI appears most often and usually earliest. But AI works with data: if contact history is incomplete and arrangements remain in emails, there is nothing reliable to work with. In this area, AI therefore follows process organisation rather than replacing it.

This does not mean one must wait until the other is fully complete. It means that sequence affects the result, and we establish that sequence for a specific process at the outset.

Commercial decisions and customer-facing communication remain with the team. We treat this as a design principle that shapes the scope, not as a configuration setting.
